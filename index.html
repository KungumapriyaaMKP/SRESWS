Our Heritage

Three generations at the loom, one promise of purity.

In 1962, Sri Ramanathan Iyer set up four pit looms behind his home on Gandhi Road. He had one rule: no saree would leave his house unless he would gift it to his own mother.

Today, more than four hundred weaving families across Kanchipuram, Varanasi and Mysore work with us. The rule has not changed.

The Craft

From cocoon to heirloom, in four acts.

  1. 01

    Reeling the silk

    Mulberry cocoons from Karnataka are boiled and reeled by hand. Three fine filaments are twisted into one, giving Kanjivaram its weight.

  2. 02

    Dyeing in the river

    Hanks are dyed in copper vats, then washed in the Vegavathi. Its mineral water is said to give our reds their depth.

  3. 03

    Drawing the zari

    Pure silver wire is drawn, flattened, wound around silk and electroplated with 24 carat gold. Every reel is tested before it touches the loom.

  4. 04

    Weaving by hand

    Two weavers share one pit loom for up to five weeks. The border and body are woven separately and interlocked with the korvai technique.
